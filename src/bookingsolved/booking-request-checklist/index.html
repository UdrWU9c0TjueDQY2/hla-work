What is explicit in the request?

Mark each detail as clear, missing or not applicable. “Not applicable” excludes it from the follow-up; it does not verify it.

Sample request: “Could I book a 45-minute lash fill at the studio on Friday afternoon?” The service, length and place are clear. Ask for the date and start time before offering a slot.

Can this appointment move?

When a client asks for a different time, keep the original appointment on the record while you check two replacements. This one-page worksheet has space for the old time, the two options and the final decision.

Download the appointment move worksheet (PDF)

Read the worksheet and worked example
  1. Write the current appointment’s date, start and finish. Note the requested date, service length and cleanup time. An anonymous booking reference is enough.
  2. Find the earliest time the preceding service and its cleanup are finished. Note the next fixed commitment. For each proposed start, add service minutes, then cleanup minutes. Both must fit inside that window.
  3. Record the state: offered, accepted, declined or still checking. Recheck the live calendar before making a change. If the move is accepted, record the agreed replacement and when the old slot closes.

Example: Move Tuesday’s 09:00 appointment to Thursday. Thursday opens at 14:15, and the next booking begins at 15:15. The service takes 40 minutes plus 10 minutes of cleanup. A 14:20 start finishes cleanup at 15:10 and fits. A 14:30 start finishes cleanup at 15:20 and overlaps the next booking by five minutes.

MAKE THE RESULT USEFUL

Do not fill ambiguity with an assumption.

“Friday afternoon” leaves the date and time open. “The usual” can leave the service and duration open. A location mentioned last month may no longer apply. Ask a focused question before adding an event.

Once details are clear, separately check availability, travel or setup time, and your own booking policies. This checklist cannot decide whether a booking is accepted or whether the client has agreed to a policy.

Use the generated message as a draft. Remove questions that your context already answers, and review the tone before sending it yourself.

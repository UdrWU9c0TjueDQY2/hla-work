TWO SHIFTS / ONE TIMELINE

One shift ends at 6.
The next starts at 5:30.

That is a 30-minute overlap—even when the first shift began yesterday. Compare the dates and times before copying both entries into a work log.

Wall-clock comparison in one time zone. Do not use across a daylight-saving change or travel between time zones. This is not a payroll or scheduling-compliance decision.

MAKE THE RESULT USEFUL

Overlap is a flag to investigate, not hours to delete.

Check whether the entries describe separate shifts, a corrected time, an on-call period or duplicate records. An overlap alone cannot tell you which entry is correct or what should be paid.

If one shift ends exactly when the other starts, the result is touching, with zero gap. This is different from an overlap and does not prove that the schedule allows enough travel, breaks or rest.

For an overnight shift, include both dates. On a clock-change night, compare employer records with explicit time-zone offsets instead of relying on this wall-clock tool.

REUSABLE TEACHING DIAGRAM

See which minutes both entries contain

Adding two entry durations counts their shared interval twice. To find unique recorded clock time, add the durations and subtract their overlap once. Check what each record represents before changing it.

Read the examples

Every example starts record A at 22:00 on day one and ends it at 06:00 on day two, for 480 minutes. Record B also takes place on day two.

  • Overlap — B runs from 05:30 to 10:00 for 270 minutes. The 05:30–06:00 interval appears in both. Entry sum 750 − shared time 30 = unique recorded time 720 minutes.
  • Touching — B runs from 06:00 to 10:00 for 240 minutes. Shared time is zero. Unique recorded time is 480 + 240 = 720 minutes.
  • Gap — B runs from 07:00 to 10:00 for 180 minutes. The 06:00–07:00 gap adds no recorded time. Unique recorded time is 480 + 180 = 660 minutes, within a 720-minute overall span.

These examples use one time zone and exclude breaks and clock changes. Whether any interval is payable depends on the relevant work records and rules.

Check the idea with a contained entry

A runs 22:00–06:00. B runs 01:00–02:00 the following morning. B is fully inside A. The entry sum is 540 minutes and all 60 minutes of B are shared, giving 480 unique recorded minutes.

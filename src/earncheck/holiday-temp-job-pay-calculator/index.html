What the check looks like

The first paycheck, week three

A composite scenario, not a customer story.

Leo took a seasonal warehouse job in November: 17.50 an hour, "around 44 hours", pay every two weeks. The first statement arrives on a Friday and the gross is about 110 less than the number in his head. His head, it turns out, was wrong in two places and right in one.

Wrong place one: the pay period closed on the Saturday, so the last two shifts are in the next one. Wrong place two: the half-hour unpaid break he worked through on two nights was still deducted, because nobody told payroll. Right place: four overtime hours were paid at straight time because his employer's workweek runs Sunday to Saturday and his hours straddled two of them, which is legal, and which the calculator above would have shown him if he had set the workweek first.

He confirms each shift in EarnCheck, types the statement figures, and gets Regular: Match, Overtime: Difference to Review, 35.00. He reads the "explanations to check first", works out the workweek thing himself, and sends nothing. The breaks he raises with his lead on Monday, using the neutral message the app drafted, edited down to two lines. The next statement matches.

Who it is not for

  • Salaried or exempt staff. The overtime rules here are for hourly, non-exempt work.
  • Anyone who wants net pay. Taxes and deductions are deliberately out of scope, in the app and on this page.
  • Tipped work where tips are most of the money. EarnCheck estimates gross from hours and rules; it does not track tips.
  • Anyone looking for legal advice. A difference is something to review. Your payroll team or local labour authority confirms what applies.

  1. Hours worked Confirmed shifts, minus unpaid breaks. Everything starts here, which is why an independent record matters.
  2. Regular pay Hours up to the overtime threshold, times your rate.
  3. Overtime pay Hours over the threshold, times 1.5 under the U.S. federal rule or Ontario's 44-hour rule. Your contract or state may differ.
  4. Premiums Night, weekend or holiday extras, if your employer pays them. Many seasonal jobs do not; check the offer letter.
  5. Gross pay Regular plus overtime plus premiums. This is the number EarnCheck estimates and compares.
  6. Deductions Tax, pension and the rest. Kept separate on purpose: a deduction is not missing pay.
  7. Net pay What lands in the account. Last on the stub, first thing you look at, least useful for spotting an hours error.

Questions seasonal workers ask

How is overtime calculated for a seasonal job?

In the U.S., the federal Fair Labor Standards Act requires time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek for covered, non-exempt employees; some states add daily rules. In Ontario it is hours over 44 in a week. The calculator uses whichever you pick. Your workweek may not match your pay period, which is where a lot of "my overtime is wrong" turns out to be "my week starts on Sunday".

Why does my first paycheck look short?

Usually one of four things: the pay period closed before your last shifts, unpaid breaks were deducted, the hourly rate differs from the offer, or deductions were larger than expected. Compare gross with gross before looking at net; a tax deduction is not missing pay. EarnCheck marks each line Match, Difference to Review or Incomplete Information so you look at the right one.

Do I get holiday pay as a temp?

It depends on where you are and the job. Public holiday pay rules differ by country and state or province, and many seasonal contracts pay a flat rate with no premium. The calculator has a premium field so you can model what your offer letter actually says; it does not assume one.

Does EarnCheck connect to my employer or bank?

No. No account, no bank login, no employer login. You confirm shifts and type figures from your pay statement; records stay on the phone unless you export them. It never contacts your employer; it drafts a neutral message you can send yourself if a difference holds up.

Is it free?

Free covers one job and a complete first pay period, which is most of a seasonal job. Pro adds unlimited pay periods, multiple jobs, advanced overtime and premium rules, and full exports, on monthly or annual plans with a trial on the annual plan for eligible customers, per the listing.

Is a difference proof my employer underpaid me?

No, and the app says so too: a difference is something to review, not proof of an error. Start with the explanations to check first. Your payroll team or local labour authority can confirm what applies to you. This page is not legal advice.

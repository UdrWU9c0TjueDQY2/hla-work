SIX COMMON QUESTIONS

Draw the line in words.

These are starting prompts, not a complete checklist for every trade. Use the notes to name quantities, specifications and boundaries. Selecting a category does not record customer agreement.

Your discussion notes



“Materials included” is only a start.
It still leaves questions about product, quantity, finish and substitutions. Write the specific detail you know, or name the question you still need answered.
“Client responsibility” needs confirmation.
Do not silently assume someone will clear the room or supply an item. Make that handoff explicit and check that it is practical before relying on it.
Separate extra work.
If a new request changes the scope, keep a record of what changed and revisit the price and timing through your normal process.
Define the finish.
“Cleanup” can mean several things. Describe the intended condition of the workspace and anything deliberately left outside this proposal.

Is this a contract or a formal quote?
No. It is a worksheet for organizing discussion notes. It contains no price, acceptance record or trade-specific contract terms.
Does “unclassified” mean the job is risky?
No. The count only reports how many dropdowns still say “To confirm.” It is not a risk assessment or quality score.
What if a question does not apply?
Explain that in its notes. The six prompts are deliberately broad; your actual work may need other questions.
Are my notes saved?
No. The tool does not submit or store entries. Copying places the text on your clipboard, and you decide whether to share it.

    Dates before a renewal

    When should you review this subscription?

    Enter the renewal date and the notice rule shown by your provider. Choose how many days earlier you want to review the plan. No account or subscription name is needed.

    The file contains one all-day review event. Import it into your calendar if you want it there. It sends no notice, sets no alarm and makes no account changes.

    Example: A March 1, 2027 renewal with 30 calendar days of notice gives January 30. Reviewing seven days earlier gives January 23.     Find the biller
    before the button.

    The service you use and the company that bills you may be different. For an Apple-billed subscription, Apple provides the management route. A plan purchased from another company may need to be managed with that company.

    If you cannot find an expected subscription in Apple settings, check the receipt and which Apple Account it names. If there is no Apple receipt, identify the billing company from your own records. Apple's subscription cancellation guide explains these cases. This page does not access those records for you.

    Do not treat a missing listing in one account as proof that the subscription ended. Confirm the account and provider before marking your own record as resolved.

    Keep three dates
    separate.

    A useful review distinguishes the next charge, the date you plan to act and the end of access after a change. They can be different dates. Record the provider's wording where the timing is unclear.

    Next scheduled charge
    The provider's current billing date.
    Planned action date
    Your own reminder to review or use the provider's route.
    Confirmed access end
    The date the provider states after a change, if one is supplied.

    Checking a cancellation box in your notes is not evidence from the provider. Keep the confirmation separately, and distinguish a request sent from a cancellation confirmed. Refund eligibility is a separate question.

    A review that ends
    with a usable note.

    1. Gather the facts. Review one plan using the six prompts. Leave unknown details marked unknown rather than estimating a deadline.
    2. Choose your own action. Keeping a useful plan can be a reasonable outcome. If you are undecided, record what you still need to check and when you will revisit it.
    3. Use the provider's official route. If you choose to change or cancel, take that step there. This checklist cannot submit it.
    4. Keep the outcome. Note whether you requested a change, received confirmation or still need a response. Check the later account state against what the provider said.

    For a quick private record, write: service, account, billing provider, price and cycle, next charge, decision, action taken, confirmation and next check. Keep currencies separate when you later compare costs.

Who should pick a subscription tracker that doesn't link to your bank, and who shouldn't

If you want privacy, no login and deadlines a bank feed cannot see, a manual tracker fits. Two cases where MoneyShield is the wrong pick:

You want charges found for you

If you would rather an app read your statements and surface every recurring payment without typing, pick a bank-linked tracker. Rocket Money is the most established one on the list; Feenko and Hiatus do the same job. You give up a degree of privacy for that convenience, and you should read how each one handles your login.

You want a whole budget

MoneyShield tracks money deadlines: subscriptions, trials, returns, refunds, warranties and bills. It is not a budgeting app. If you want envelopes, categories and net worth, look at a full budget app instead and use a tracker only if the budget app's subscription view is thin.

Honest limits of a manual tracker

  • It only knows what you tell it. A subscription you never enter will never be reminded. Setup time depends on how many items you add; use a separate statement review to look for anything you missed.
  • Totals are estimates. Monthly and yearly figures are built from the prices you entered. MoneyShield labels them as estimates in the app, and currencies are kept separate rather than converted.
  • Some of MoneyShield's features sit in the Plus tier. The free plan has limits on deadlines and subscriptions; unlimited entries, alerts 30 days ahead, the full Cancel First ranking, saved What If scenarios and export are Plus features. Whether the free limits fit depends on how many items and alert dates you need.
  • Nothing here cancels for you. MoneyShield can point to the cancellation route and lets you record the outcome; you still complete and verify the cancellation yourself.

Bank-linked vs manual: what you trade

Bank-linked (Rocket Money, Hiatus, Feenko)

  • Describes finding recurring charges from connected transactions
  • Requires financial-account access through the app's connection flow
  • May bundle budgeting, credit or bill-negotiation features; check the current listing
  • Review the current price and data disclosures before connecting

Manual (MoneyShield, Bobby, Subscription Manager, Subpilot, MonoBoost)

  • Does not require a linked bank feed according to the listings checked
  • Requires manual entry up front; reminders only cover items you supplied
  • Can record a future deadline, such as a return window or trial end, before a charge appears
  • Misses anything you forget to enter; check each app's privacy disclosures separately

A fair summary: bank-linked listings emphasize automatic discovery from connected transactions. Manual listings emphasize user-entered items and can record a future deadline before a charge appears. Compare each app's current privacy disclosures and features.

Questions people ask before choosing

Do I need to connect my bank to track subscriptions?

No. Manual trackers like MoneyShield, Bobby and Subscription Manager work from details you type in, such as name, price, billing cycle and next charge date. You trade automatic detection for manual entry without a linked bank feed. Check each app's current privacy disclosures separately.

Is MoneyShield free?

Yes to download and use. The listing describes a free plan plus an optional Plus tier: $3.99 a month, $24.99 a year or $59.99 once (US prices). New installs start with 7 days of the full feature set, then move to the free plan automatically with nothing billed.

How does a manual tracker catch a renewal I forgot?

You enter the subscription once with its next charge date. The app then reminds you before that date (MoneyShield offers same day, 1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days before, with 30 days in the Plus tier). The reminder can only cover an item you entered.

Which app actually cancels subscriptions for me?

None of the manual apps cancel anything on your behalf; they point you to the right place. Rocket Money's listing says it can cancel subscriptions for you as part of its service. MoneyShield's Cancellation Assistant shows a route, gives a checklist and request templates, and reminds you to confirm it went through.

Does the FTC click-to-cancel rule protect me now?

Not yet. The FTC finalised a rule in October 2024 requiring cancellation to be as easy as sign-up, but the US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated it in July 2025, days before it was due to take effect. Existing law (ROSCA) still applies. Apple publishes current steps for cancelling Apple-billed subscriptions in iPhone Settings.

Can I track returns, refunds and free trials too?

MoneyShield does: its deadline types are Free Trial, Subscription, Return, Refund, Warranty, Bill and Other. Most of the other trackers on this page focus on subscriptions and bills only.

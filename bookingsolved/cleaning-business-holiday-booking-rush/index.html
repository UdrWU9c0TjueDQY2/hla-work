Three ways to run the rush

The notes app

Every request copied into a list, the list reconciled with the calendar at night. Works until Thursday, when the list and the calendar disagree and nobody knows which one is right.

The booking portal

Clients can choose an available time in a portal. In this example, Dana’s regular clients also text her, so she would need to reconcile requests from both places.

Check before you reply

Clients keep texting. You keep Apple Calendar. The only change is that the calendar gets checked while the message is open, and the reply goes out with a time already saved. That is what BookingSolved does, and the whole story below is that one habit.

Dana’s week is an original fictional example. The conversations and calendar illustrate booking decisions.

Three people text Dana on Monday. Karen wants the house ready for guests and can do any day except Wednesday. Priya wants a deep clean before a Friday party. Mike needs the lake house cleaned between an 11 a.m. checkout and a 4 p.m. check-in.

Karen has suggested days without picking a start time. Dana checks her calendar and the 30-minute travel buffer she uses in this example. Karen asks about Thursday morning, and they agree on 9 a.m. Dana reviews the booking before saving it and sends the confirmation herself.

Tuesday and Wednesday: when two requests change

On Tuesday evening Priya asks to move her Friday cleaning an hour later. Dana checks the new time before replying. On Wednesday Mike changes the rental checkout to Friday. His 11:30 a.m. turnover would conflict with the later Friday slot Priya requested.

Dana offers Priya a different opening, Thursday at 1:30 p.m. It leaves time after Karen’s morning clean and still gets the house ready for Friday’s party. Once Priya agrees, Dana reviews and approves the reschedule, edits her confirmation and sends it. Friday now has two separate jobs: Tom and Ali in the morning and Mike’s turnover at 11:30.

On Wednesday night Karen asks whether Thursday morning is still on. Dana checks the saved appointment and replies with the agreed 9 a.m. start.

Questions cleaners ask

When does the holiday cleaning rush actually start?

The timing depends on the clients and location. Protectivity’s Christmas guide suggests promoting availability four to six weeks ahead and keeping some time open for short-notice jobs. Speed Cleaning’s Thanksgiving guide suggests using past bookings to estimate demand.

How do I stop double-booking during the rush?

Use one calendar for confirmed jobs. For a message like “Thursday morning,” check the service length and travel time, agree on a specific start, and record the booking. In this example Dana confirms Thursday at 9 a.m. with Karen.

Should I use a booking portal for my cleaning business?

A portal lets clients choose a time themselves. In this fictional week Dana’s clients send texts, so she checks each proposed booking against Apple Calendar before replying. BookingSolved reads only a request she selects, and she sends each reply herself.

Does BookingSolved send the confirmation for me?

No. It writes an editable reply in a friendly, professional or concise tone, and you send it yourself from whatever app the client used. Nothing goes to a client without you tapping send.

What happens when a client changes the time, like Priya did?

Dana checks Priya’s requested change against Friday’s other work. She offers Thursday at 1:30 p.m., confirms it with Priya and then approves the calendar change. BookingSolved shows a preview before a reschedule is saved.

How it works, in three steps

  1. Add the request. Paste the text, pick a screenshot, or use the Share Sheet. Only what you choose is read; the app never watches your messages in the background.
  2. Review what it found. Service, date, time and duration are worked out on the phone. Anything vague, like "next Friday" or "any day except Wednesday", is flagged, not guessed.
  3. Approve and reply. It checks Apple Calendar, prepares the booking, reschedule or cancellation, and writes an editable reply. You approve, you send. Nothing changes in Calendar until you say so.

Who it is not for

  • Teams with a dispatcher and a shared schedule. This is a one-person tool built around one person's calendar.
  • Anyone who wants clients to self-book from a public link. There is no portal, by design.
  • Google Calendar or Outlook households. Apple Calendar is the source of truth, and that is the only calendar it talks to.
  • Businesses that need deposits or card holds at booking. No prices are stored, and no money moves.

Honest limits

BookingSolved will not fix a rush you have already overbooked, and it cannot tell you what to charge for a same-day after-party clean. Protectivity's guide to the Christmas cleaning rush covers that side: promote availability four to six weeks out, keep a couple of slots open each week for urgent work, and charge a premium for it. Speed Cleaning's Thanksgiving rush notes are a fair companion for the earlier peak. What the app does is narrower: it closes the gap between a text and a calendar entry, which is where most December double-bookings are born. If your calendar lives on your iPhone, that gap is worth closing before the fifteenth.

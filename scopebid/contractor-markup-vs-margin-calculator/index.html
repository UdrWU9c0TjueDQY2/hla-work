How $400 becomes 40% or 66.7%

Follow a $1,000 quote with $600 in direct costs through the calculator. Both percentages start with the same $400 of gross profit.

20 seconds · Silent, with on-screen captions · Fictional Canadian-dollar example

Read the video transcript
  1. Enter a customer price of $1,000 before tax, $600 for labour and materials, and zero discount.
  2. Subtract the $600 cost from the $1,000 price. That leaves $400 gross profit before overhead.
  3. For gross margin, divide $400 by the $1,000 customer price. The result is 40%. Forty cents of each sales dollar remains after direct costs.
  4. For markup, divide the same $400 by the $600 direct cost. The result rounds to 66.7%. The smaller starting number gives the larger percentage.

Plain-language answers

Margin questions

What is the difference between markup and gross margin?

Gross profit is customer price before tax minus direct cost. Markup divides that profit by direct cost. Gross margin divides it by the customer price. With a $150 price and $100 cost, profit is $50, markup is 50%, and margin is 33.3%.

Why can a 10% discount cut margin by more than 10%?

The discount comes entirely out of the price, while the labour and materials may be unchanged. On the sample $1,200 quote with $650 direct cost, a 10% discount changes price to $1,080 and profit from $550 to $430. Gross margin falls from 45.8% to 39.8%—a drop of 6 percentage points.

Does the calculator include overhead or take-home pay?

No. “Gross profit” here means price before tax minus the direct cost entered. It is not net profit or take-home pay. Rent, insurance, travel, admin time, tools, payment fees and income tax may still need to be covered.

Does it calculate sales tax or GST/HST?

No. Enter the customer price before tax. Sales tax is not treated as gross profit. You set and remain responsible for your own tax rates and compliance.

Is the target-margin price a recommended quote?

No. The minimum price is the first whole-cent amount at or above the chosen gross-margin percentage. With zero direct cost, every positive price has 100% gross margin, so a lower target has no exact solution. It does not know your overhead, local market, minimum charge, scope risk, contract, tax obligations or business goals.
